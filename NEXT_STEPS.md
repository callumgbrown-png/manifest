## Questions for Dave
- **Is progression rewarding enough?** In simulation the median company only grows from 16,000 to about 17,000 net worth over a season (top bots in a 10-company world reach ~24,000). Options: (a) leave it and see how real players feel, (b) raise rewards by lowering tax from 7% to 4–5% (simulate first to keep inflation in check), (c) add season goals or achievements so progress isn't only net worth. Recommendation: (a) plus a short tester survey question about it.
- **Dock alerts when the game tab is closed (Web Push)?** This is the only other unticked item, and it's blocked on you. It needs a service worker, a VAPID key pair kept as a server secret, the free `web-push` package, and storing a push subscription per player (a browser endpoint, which counts as personal data and would belong in the privacy notice from the email question). Alerts already work while the tab is open in the background. Options: (a) skip it for the closed test, (b) build it as opt-in with a "turn off alerts" button that deletes the stored subscription, (c) build it together with option (b) of the email question (privacy notice plus delete-account). Recommendation: (a) for now; revisit with the privacy notice. (Added by the scheduled run, 29 Oct.)
- **Small worlds drift down about 1% a day.** With market scaling, 20–100 companies now lose ~1%/day of money (it was +8%/day inflation before). Options: (a) accept it and lower tax from the admin page during the closed test if players feel poor, (b) scale tax and port fee down for small worlds too. Recommendation: (a), decide after real players try it.

- **Fuel credit has no limit.** A company's last ship can always sail with fuel on credit, so a player who keeps sailing empty can go deep into debt: in the season playtest, pressing "Set sail" on the old default every 4 hours ended at about -71,000. The game no longer suggests empty trips that don't pay (29 Oct), but nothing stops it by hand. Options: (a) cap credit, e.g. no new fuel credit below -2,000 cash, (b) leave it. Recommendation: (a). **Also (30 Oct run):** once a one-ship company's cash is below zero it can never trade again that season: cargo must be paid for in cash, and nothing else earns money (unless goods it listed earlier sell). A test company that sailed empty 17 times sat at -2,513 with 37 days left. The game now says so plainly instead of "every company is waiting, check back in a few hours". If you want broke players to stay in the season, options: (c) let the last ship load a small cargo on credit too (e.g. up to cash + 2,000), (d) a one-off "rescue loan" with interest. Recommendation: (a) plus (c), so debt is capped but a broke player can still climb back.
- **Easy may now be too easy.** With the new trip ranking, playtest players on Easy who follow the advice finish around 50,000 (bots about 20,000). Options: (a) leave it, Easy is meant to be generous, (b) raise Easy's tax or fees a little. Recommendation: (a) for the closed test, then see.

- **Buy orders cost money and give nothing back.** When another player fills your buy order, you pay the full price, the seller is paid, and the goods vanish at the port ("used up"). The buyer gets no cargo and nothing on the books, so net worth drops by the order's full value (now pinned by a test). A new player who tries the Orders tab just loses money; a rational one never posts one. The Orders tab now warns about this plainly (29 Oct). Options: (a) deliver filled goods to the buyer as a listing at that port, which their own ship can pick up or relist (reuses sell-order code, no new rules), (b) hide buy orders until warehouses exist, (c) leave as is with the warning. Recommendation: (a).

- **Players are logged out at every new season.** Starting a new season deletes every session, so everyone online sees "A new season has started. Log in again." and must re-enter a password they may not have typed for weeks; there is no password reset (no emails). Accounts already carry over, so sessions could too. Options: (a) keep sessions across seasons and just reload each player's new company, (b) leave it. Recommendation: (a); it's a small change in `newSeason()` but touches accounts, so asking first. (Found by the scheduled run, 29 Oct.)
- **Hard seasons start dead for about a day.** At hour 0 of a Hard season no trip pays anywhere (9% tax and the 40 port fee eat the 20% pre-stock spread); in a 30-bot engine run the first paying port appeared at hour 22, and over a whole Hard season 25 of 134 start-port checks found nothing paying over +50 (Normal: 1, Easy: 0). Hard playtest medians: advice 16,301, plan 21,465, bots 18,458, so players following the plain advice barely beat the 16,000 start. Options: (a) leave it, Hard is meant to be hard, (b) a bigger `start_spread` on Hard (e.g. 0.3, simulate first), (c) per-difficulty spread in the presets. Recommendation: (a) for the closed test (start on Easy/Normal), revisit (c) if anyone plays Hard. (Found by the scheduled run, 30 Oct.)
- **Buying a second ship is a trap for most players.** Upkeep is charged at the start of each day, and if cash is then below zero an idle spare ship is sold at half price (6,000 lost). A scripted Easy player who followed the advice and bought a ship whenever cash passed 14,000 lost a ship this way 5 times in one season and finished at 13,396 (down from 16,000) despite ~32,000 of trading profit; cash was below zero only briefly, while thousands sat in cargo. The advice now keeps a day's upkeep in reserve and the buy button warns (30 Oct), and the same player then finished at 44,007 with 1 forced sale. Options: (a) only force a sale when cash plus cargo at cost is below zero, (b) give a day's grace (sell only if still below zero at the next upkeep), (c) leave it with the new warnings. Recommendation: (b), simple and still stops debt piling up. (Found by the scheduled run, 30 Oct.)
